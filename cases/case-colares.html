Colares Flap

1977–1978 · Pará, Brazil

← Back to all Important UFO Cases
Date
Late 1977 – early 1978
Location
Colares, Pará, Brazil
Type
CE2 — Multiple witness injuries

Description

Between late 1977 and early 1978, the small island community of Colares on the coast of Pará state in northern Brazil experienced a wave — a "flap" — of UAP activity that remains one of the most thoroughly documented and disturbing cases in the history of the phenomenon.

Residents reported seeing luminous objects of various shapes — some described as discs, others as cylinders or "chandeliers" — moving at low altitude over the island, particularly at night. Dozens of witnesses reported being struck by beams of light emanating from the objects. The beams produced burns, puncture-like marks, and in some cases a lasting weakness or fatigue. Local health clinics documented injuries that could not be explained by known causes.

The situation became severe enough that the mayor of Colares formally requested assistance from the Brazilian Air Force. In response, the Air Force launched Operação Prato ("Operation Saucer"), a formal investigation that dispatched a team of military personnel — including Captain Uyrangê Bolivar Soares Nogueira de Hollanda Lima — to the region. Over several months the team produced hundreds of photographs, sketches, and motion picture footage of the objects, along with extensive written reports.

When Operação Prato concluded in 1978, its findings were classified by the Brazilian military and remained so for nearly two decades. The documents and film footage were only partially declassified in the 1990s and 2000s. In 1997, Captain Uyrangê gave an extended television interview describing his experiences during the operation, including his own sighting of a large object and his belief that the phenomenon was real and unexplained. He died under disputed circumstances shortly after the interview aired.

Colares is significant both for the volume of physical evidence documented by a national military investigation and for the reported physiological effects on witnesses — a category of UAP incident often compared to the 1980 Cash-Landrum case in Texas and the 1967 Falcon Lake case in Canada.
